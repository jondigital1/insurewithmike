## Mistakes made. Do not repeat these

1. **"The PUF has no rates for dental plans." Wrong.** The join was matching on
   the 16-character PLAN ID; `NJRates` keys on the 14-character STANDARD
   COMPONENT ID. The same bug showed zero rate rows for medical plans too, which
   is what exposed it. Always sanity-check a join by confirming the known-good
   side also matches.

2. **"The pediatric dental gap is large enough to reorder the shortlist."
   Overstated.** A sweep of 84 households with children, across 4 family shapes,
   3 utilisation levels and 7 income bands: the top pick changed 0 times, and
   shortlist composition changed in 32 of 84. This is a wrong-total fix, not a
   ranking fix. Do not re-inflate the claim.

3. **"Add dental questions to the intake." Wrong**, and it broke the project's
   own rule that a reveal is justified only by a genuinely different follow-up.
   Dental needs no questions at all.

4. **"Spend some of Mike's validation session on dental." Reversed.** Mike has a
   very large feature wishlist. His requests are evidence about the territory,
   not a roadmap. The scarce resource is his attention on the one question that
   matters: is the medical shortlist any good.

5. **Two measurement bugs in `scripts/question-value.ts`**, both introduced and
   then fixed, both documented in the file. First: `expected_income` is derived
   in the browser from the income boxes, so mutating a wage box without
   recomputing the total made the entire income half of the questionnaire look
   inert. Second: conditional questions looked dead because no base household
   opened their gate. Watch for the same class of error in any future
   perturbation script.

6. **`current_insurer` and `current_insurer_feeling` measure as inert in that
   script only** because it scores the ranking, and they feed the staying-put
   option on a path the script does not read. That is an artefact, not a verdict.

7. **"Remote intake inverts the client code flow, so the code must be minted
   before the form and travel inside the link." Wrong**, and it would have been
   caught by reading `issueClientCode` properly. The code mints on demand with
   no pre-existing row required, and its alphabet drops O against 0 and I and L
   against 1 specifically so a code survives being read down a phone. That is a
   token designed to be relayed by a person after the fact, which is exactly the
   flow that was already intended. The thanks page agrees: code card, copy
   button, clipboard fallback, re-display on return visits. The design was
   already correct and the proposed change would have made it worse. Read the
   thing the design already says before proposing to invert it.

## Dental and vision data audit, 10 August 2026

Run in answer to two questions from Mike relayed on 10 August: can we get adult
vision rates, and do we hold all the dental plan and rate information. Four
independent agents: a dental completeness audit, a vision sweep of the local
filings, a web researcher on vision rate availability, and a second researcher

### Dental: complete for premium quoting, with three named caveats

The rate data is complete and clean. Every one of the 28 plans files the same
51 age labels (0-14, single years 15 to 63, 64 and over) with no holes; the row
math reconciles exactly (25 plans x 51 ages, plus 3 Horizon plans x 51 x 6
rating areas, = 2,193 rows). Rates are provably flat across rating areas and
tobacco. Benefits are uniformly 9 lines per plan, 252 rows, with exclusion and
limit text carried through. Individual and family deductible and MOOP are filed
and read for all 28.

Three caveats were found. The first two were wrong-answer bugs, and both were
FIXED and verified the same day:

1. **County availability, fixed.** The dental rows of NJServiceAreas now load
   (`loadServiceAreas` gained a dentalOnly switch), `DentalPlan` carries its
   `serviceAreaId`, and `dentalSoldIn` in dental.ts gates the dental view, per
   plan rather than per issuer because 8 of 28 dental plans are county-limited
   while sibling plans from the same issuer are statewide. Verified: Bergen
   drops exactly the four 35152 Select Plans, Somerset drops 7 (adding
   EssentialSmile and both Dentegra plans), Salem drops 5. The dropped plans
   are named in an agent-only line so the shorter list does not read as
   missing data. `cheapestPediatricAnnual` applies the same gate, so the
   pediatric dental gap is now the floor the household's county can buy.
2. **The 3-child rating cap, fixed.** `quoteDentalPlan` now rates the three
   oldest children and carries the rest as `freeChildAges`, mirroring
   `monthlyListPremium` in premium.ts and the filed NJBusinessRules MAX
   CHILDREN IN POLICY = 3. Verified: ages 16/14/12/9/6 rate as 16/14/12 with
   9 and 6 free, and a five-child floor equals the three-child floor. The
   dental view says so in a visible line, since it explains a price the
   client is looking at. The two-child floor is unchanged at $348.72 and the
   cheapest child plan is statewide, so medical totals for typical households
   did not move.
3. **Plan-level exclusion text is still dropped**, including the filed warning
   that the $450 MOOP applies to children only on the four Choice PPO plans,
   so the loaded MOOP overstates adult protection there. Open, smallest of the
   three.

Also true: the filings simply do not contain dental coinsurance schedules,
adult annual benefit maximums, or waiting periods. If out-of-pocket dental
modelling is ever wanted, it needs carrier plan documents, the same way medical
copays needed the SBCs. Nothing in the rate or benefit files is being lost.

### Adult vision rates: obtainable by an agent, not as a dataset

- **No public machine-readable rate source exists, and none can under current
  law.** Standalone vision is an excepted benefit under 45 CFR 148.220(b)(1);
  the ACA's standalone-plan exchange path covers dental only, which is why the
  PUF holds 28 dental plans and zero vision. A second researcher tasked purely
  with refuting this could not: closest misses were a UnitedHealthOne brochure
  (below), FEDVIP's structured tables (federal employees only), and SERFF NJ
  (public but per-filing PDFs behind a click-through that forbids automation).
- **The market is flat-rated, which shrinks the problem.** Individual vision is
  priced as flat per-state monthly rates, no age bands, no county rating. The
  whole NJ market is roughly 15 to 20 numbers across VSP, EyeMed,
  UnitedHealthOne, Humana, Ameritas and Delta.
- **Range: roughly $5 to $35 per adult per month.** The only carrier found
  publishing literal NJ figures in a public document is UnitedHealthOne: Plan A
  $10.40 plus $6.60 per dependent, Plan B $14.30 plus $9.00, stamped as of
  25 June 2021, so stale and needing a current pull before any figure ships.
- **The practical route is appointment, not integration.** VSP's individual
  broker program (8 percent commission) and EyeMed's (10 percent) give Mike
  portals and client enrolment links showing live NJ rates; one Direct Benefits
  appointment covers VSP, EyeMed and Ameritas together. No agent-accessible
  quoting API exists for this product line; Ideon licenses the data at
  enterprise pricing only. SERFF Filing Access NJ (TOI H20I) is the citable
  backstop for verifying a filed rate, manually.
- **What the no-fake-data rule permits:** a static NJ vision rate card, each
  row stamped with source and as-of date, populated only from figures actually
  observed, refreshed on a calendar; plus links out to Mike's own broker
  enrolment pages for the exact price. No scraping, no live quoting, no
  estimates dressed as quotes.

## Server work: decided 10 August 2026

There will be a server and a database. It is not next in the queue, but the
decisions below were settled in conversation on 10 August and are recorded here
so the schema is not designed against a different set of assumptions. Nothing
here is built.

### Decision 1: outbound prospect contact goes out from the CRM, never from us

Mike asked whether the system could text a prospect the intake link. It should
not, and the send stays in his CRM under his own name and number. Four reasons,
in order of weight:

1. **It keeps phone numbers out of our database.** The question was never
   whether we have somewhere to put one. A record holding household answers
   keyed to an opaque code and a record holding a direct-contact identifier are
   different regulatory postures and different breach stories. Sending the
   message ourselves means storing the field that crosses that line.
2. **An unknown number asking someone to click a link and enter health details
   is indistinguishable from phishing**, because that is what the phishing looks
   like. From Mike's number, in a thread the prospect recognises, after a call
   they remember, it reads as legitimate. Opt-outs are the symptom; the trust
   gap is the disease.
3. **It keeps delivery channel-agnostic.** Build the submission endpoint and a
   link, and CRM, email, or reading a URL over the phone are all the same to us.
   Sending SMS ourselves couples the product to one channel and makes us the
   compliance surface for it.
4. **Cost.** 10DLC brand and campaign registration is carrier-side and takes
   weeks, plus consent records, STOP and HELP handling, quiet hours and delivery
   webhooks. A message template in his CRM is an afternoon.

### Decision 2: one general intake link, no code required to start

The flow is: the agent collects PII into the CRM first, the CRM sends the
invitation with a single general link that is identical for every prospect, the
prospect fills the survey anonymously, the system mints the code at submission,
and the prospect relays that code back to the agent.

This is the flow the existing code was built for. It is also the stronger
position on the project's own principle: with a general link our system never
learns that a prospect exists until an anonymous submission arrives. A
per-prospect coded link would mean issuing a token bound to a named person
before they have done anything, and a forwarded invitation would hand away
someone else's slot.

The intake collects no DOB, SSN, phone or email, and that does not change. Those
are collected by the agent into the CRM before the invitation goes out, which is
the system built to hold them.

Addendum, decided later on 10 August with the naming: **each agent gets their
own Before We Quote address** (per-agent subdomain or equivalent). This makes
the general link per-agency without changing the flow: the address the
prospect clicks is what routes the anonymous submission to the right agency,
the code still mints at submission, and the prospect still relays it. The
schema already scopes codes and submissions per agency, so this lands without
a redesign. Vercel supports wildcard subdomains when the time comes.

### Decision 3: notify the agent on submission, by SMS and email

Employees, not prospects, so the consent and opt-out objections in Decision 1 do
not apply. This is the cheapest item on the list and it fills a real hole, since
nothing currently tells an agent that a submission landed.

The message body carries the client code and nothing else.

- **Not `client_code.label`.** The schema comment gives "Alvarez renewal" as the
  example value. That is a client surname one column away from the code, and
  interpolating the row for readability would put it into SMS, carrier logs,
  lock screens and mail retention. Under Decision 2 the label should be null
  anyway, since nothing is issued in advance. Guard it regardless, and say why
  in a comment, because it is the obvious field to reach for.
- **Not answers, household composition, or plan results.**

SMS is the nudge. Email is the same facts plus a link into the authenticated
view and the submitted-at timestamp. Both stay at "one landed, here is the
reference, go look". The dashboard is the source of truth and the notification
is a convenience, because a failed SMS must not mean a lost application.

### Decision 4: multi-tenant shape, recommended 10 August with the naming

EnrollAsst signs brokerages; brokerages have agents; every intake, code and
submission belongs to exactly one brokerage. The recommended architecture,
discussed with Jon on 10 August:

- **One application, one database, one deployment.** A new brokerage is a row
  and a subdomain, never new infrastructure. Wildcard DNS on
  beforewequote.com (registered at GoDaddy; wildcard requires delegating
  nameservers to Vercel) routes every agent's intake link to the same app,
  and the subdomain stamps each anonymous submission with its agency. The
  schema anticipated this: client_code and submission are already scoped per
  agency.
- **Isolation by scoping, never by filtering.** A logged-in agent's requests
  carry their agency before touching data; the database is never asked for
  another brokerage's rows. Enforce at the database layer too (Postgres
  row-level security), so an application bug cannot cross tenants. UI-level
  filtering of a shared pool is the failure mode, not the design.
- **Logins are bought, not built.** A hosted auth service answers "who is
  this and which brokerage"; roles start as admin and agent, nothing finer.
- **Visibility starts whole-agency.** Everyone at a brokerage sees its full
  book, which is how Mike and Marguerite actually work. Per-agent visibility
  inside a brokerage is a later, paid-for option.
- **The no-PII rule is what keeps this calm.** Worst-case cross-tenant bug
  exposes household answers keyed to opaque codes; the identity joins live in
  each broker's own CRM and never pool.

Scale check, asked by Jon on 10 August: does this hold at national size?
Roughly 80,000 agents are registered on healthcare.gov and over a million
hold health licenses. Even 100,000 agents at a few hundred households each is
about 20 million small submissions a year, which a single scoped Postgres
handles without drama; the tenant model is the boring, proven part. What does
NOT scale yet, deliberately, is the pricing engine's New Jersey assumptions:
rating areas (most states price by county; loadRates throws on purpose if it
sees area-varying rates), tobacco loading (NJ files none, most states load up
to 50 percent), New York and Vermont's flat family tiers (which break
per-member summing entirely and need their own path), and per-state
subsidies. Going national is a state-onboarding checklist, not a rewrite,
and the ~30 healthcare.gov states share one uniform federal file format.
Two more national-scale notes: leave schema room for a grouping above the
brokerage (FMOs/IMOs are how tools actually reach thousands of agents, and
they are who EnrollAsst's real sales calls will be with), and treat open
enrolment seasonality as doctrine (near-all volume lands November to
mid-January; no risky deploys on November 1).

### Constraints that follow

- **The relay carries the attribution, not the notification.** The alert says a
  code landed, not whose it is. If three invitations went out this morning, only
  the prospect sending their code back disambiguates them. Recoverable at this
  volume, but it makes the thank-you page copy load-bearing UI rather than
  decoration, and the failure mode to design against is a prospect who finishes
  and never mentions it.
- **`consumed_at` goes vestigial.** Mint and consume land in the same request,
  so the single-use invariant in `recordSubmission` guards nothing. Fine to
  leave, but the schema currently reads as though codes are handed out in
  advance and the next person to open it will believe that. Say otherwise in the
  comment or drop the column.
- **A general link is an open write path.** Anyone can submit, and every hit
  mints a row and a code. There is no real incentive to abuse it, but rate
  limiting belongs in the server work rather than after the first junk run.
- **10DLC still applies to employee SMS.** Application-to-person messaging to US
  mobile numbers needs carrier registration regardless of the relationship. The
  low-volume tier fits comfortably and is much lighter than a marketing
  campaign, but it is not zero. Price it with the provider rather than assuming
  employees are exempt.
- **Notification routing keys off `agency_id`.** Who gets told about a
  submission is the same question as who may open it, and the schema already
  scopes everything that way. A separate recipient list would drift out of sync
  with the authorization it is supposed to mirror.
- **None of the above is a legal opinion.** Mike carries compliance obligations
  as a licensed agent and knows the messaging rules better than this document
  does. The SMS specifics are worth confirming with whoever handles that for him.
