# Onboarding questions: what to ask, why, and what each answer changes

The problem: a new user opens the app and does not know what they want to do. We
need enough about them to hand over a session they can start today, without an
interrogation that makes them close the app.

This is the research behind the question set, the question set itself, and the
tables that turn answers into one of the 24 template days already in the app.

## What the evidence constrains

**Most programming detail does not change the outcome, so do not ask about it.**
The 2026 ACSM position stand on resistance training, the first update in 17
years, pooled 137 systematic reviews. Machines versus free weights, gym versus
home, and complex periodization did not consistently change results for the
average healthy adult. What matters: about 10 sets per muscle per week as the
volume threshold, sets taken to roughly 2 to 3 reps in reserve, at least 2
sessions a week, and loads anywhere from 30 to 100% of 1RM producing similar
growth when volume and effort match. The single biggest jump is from no
resistance training to any.

Practical consequence: every question about equipment, machine preference or
split style is a filter or a preference, never a quality judgement. The app
should say so, because a user who thinks the dumbbell version is the consolation
prize trains worse.

**Adherence, not prescription, is the thing worth optimising.** In a cohort of
mobile app users, the number of distinct workout days in the first 28 days was
the strongest predictor of long term adherence, stronger than workout duration,
equipment or exercise choice. Only 18.1% of beginners were still adherent at 6
months. Dropout clusters at weeks 6 to 10 and again at 3 months. Separately, an
RCT comparing supervision formats found adherence of 88.2% supervised, 81.2%
app guided, 52.2% self guided.

Practical consequence: questions that protect early consistency (how many days
will you really come, how long have you got, what can you reach) earn their
place ahead of questions that fine tune the stimulus.

**Ask fewer questions than feels right.** Baymard's figure is 3 to 5% completion
lost per added field, and 44% abandon when the first interaction demands heavy
data entry. Showing every field at once drops completion below 20%; one question
at a time, each reading as a natural follow up, clears 40%. Perceived length
matters more than actual length.

Practical consequence: two safety taps, then four questions, then nothing until
the app has earned it.

**Self reported effort is unreliable in beginners.** Across studies, trainees
underpredict how close they are to failure: roughly 1 to 2 reps for experienced
lifters, 4 to 5 reps for less experienced ones. The recommendation in the
literature is to use the RIR scale with novices only as something to observe,
not as the thing that sets the load.

Practical consequence: the RPE box and the coach line stay hidden until the user
has either self reported 6 months of training or logged enough sessions. For
everyone else, progression runs on reps and load from the log.

**Experience is better measured behaviourally than by self label.** The
published classification model scores five things: uninterrupted training time,
time detrained, previous experience, technique, and strength level. ACSM's older
progression stand defines novice as no experience or not having trained for
several years, intermediate as about 6 months of consistent training, and sets
frequency at 2 to 3 days for novice, 3 to 4 intermediate, 4 to 5 advanced.

Practical consequence: ask "how long, without a break" and "have you trained
before", not "are you a beginner". Someone who benched for 3 years and stopped
for 2 is not a first timer and should not be given a first timer's session.

**Screening is about disease, symptoms and intended intensity, not a risk factor
audit.** ACSM's current preparticipation algorithm keys on current activity
level, known cardiovascular, metabolic or renal disease, signs or symptoms of
it, and the intensity being attempted. Risk factor profiling was deliberately
dropped to stop screening becoming a barrier to starting. The PAR-Q+ is the
validated 7 question self screen, with a yes on any item routing to follow up.

Practical consequence: two plain language questions, and a yes never locks
anyone out of a log book. It shows a "get cleared before you push hard" note and
keeps intensity defaults moderate.

**Pain needs a rule, not a referral reflex.** The pain monitoring model used in
tendinopathy rehab allows loading up to 5 out of 10 pain, with the real test
being how it feels the next morning rather than during the set. Red flags that
do warrant getting seen first are the standard list: night pain, unexplained
weight loss, numbness or pins and needles down a limb, giving way, recent
trauma, fever.

**Autonomy raises adherence.** Self determination work is consistent that choice
supports the need for autonomy, and self selected intensity produces higher
perceived autonomy and enjoyment than prescribed. So every suggestion needs a
visible "not that one" and a swap.

**Cardio is not the enemy but the modality matters.** Interference is minor at
recreational volumes. Where it shows up, running interferes with lower body
strength and size more than cycling does. Lifting first, or separating sessions
by 6 to 8 hours, is enough for almost everyone.

## The question set

Three tiers. Tier 0 and tier 1 are the whole of first run: six taps, one screen
at a time, every one skippable. Tier 2 questions surface in context later, when
the user has a reason to care. Tier 3 is never asked.

### Tier 0, the gate (2 questions, before anything else)

**G1. "Has a doctor ever told you to limit exercise, or do you have a heart,
lung, kidney or blood sugar condition?"** Yes / No / Rather not say

**G2. "When you push yourself, do you get chest pain, dizziness, blackouts or
breathlessness that seems out of proportion?"** Yes / No

Both no: straight through, no interstitial, no congratulations screen.
Either yes: a short note saying get cleared before training hard, a link to the
PAR-Q+ as the real screen, and the app continues. Effect on output: intensity
defaults sit at the lower end, suggestions never open with a heavy top set, and
the "train to failure" language is removed.

Why only two: ACSM's algorithm turns on disease, symptoms and intended
intensity. We are a log book, not a clinic, and a wall of medical questions at
first open is exactly the heavy first interaction that loses 44% of people.

### Tier 1, the four that produce a program

**Q1. "How long have you been lifting, without a break longer than a month?"**
Never done it / Under 6 months / 6 months to 2 years / Over 2 years

Behavioural, not a self label. Feeds the experience score directly and is the
strongest single input to the split choice.

**Q2. "Have you trained seriously in the past?"** (only shown if Q1 is Never or
Under 6 months)
No / Yes, within the last year / Yes, but longer ago than that

Separates the true beginner from the returner. A returner gets a beginner's
volume for 2 to 3 weeks and then their old split, not a 12 week novice ramp.

**Q3. "Realistically, how many days a week will you get there?"**
2 / 3 / 4 / 5 / 6

The word realistically is doing work. Beginners see 3 preselected and a line
saying two is enough to make progress, because early consistency is the
predictor and five days from a standing start is the classic burnout. This never
becomes a promise: the app compares it against distinct workout days logged.

**Q4. "What is this for?"**
Build muscle / Get stronger / Lean out / Stay healthy and capable

Maps onto the goal selector the app already has. Build muscle and lean out both
map to the muscle range of 6 to 12 reps, with lean out adding one honest line
that training holds the muscle and diet does the rest. There is no separate
toning program because there is no such thing.

**Q5. "Where are you training?"**
Full gym / Basic gym, dumbbells and a few machines / Home with some kit /
Bodyweight only

A filter over the 223 movement library, nothing more. The UI should say that
outright, since the evidence has no meaningful gap between equipment types.

That is six taps worst case, four for most.

### Tier 2, asked in context, never at first run

**C1. Session length.** "How long have you got today?" 30 / 45 / 60 / 75+
Asked the first time a session is started, remembered after. Sets exercise count:
30 minutes is 4 exercises, 45 is 5 to 6, 60 is 7 to 8, 75+ is 9 to 10, core
circuit included in the count.

**C2. Anything sore.** "Anything giving you trouble?" multi-select: knee, low
back, shoulder, hip, elbow, neck, wrist, nothing.
Asked after the first completed session, or immediately if the user tries to
start a lower body day and has flagged nothing yet. Each selection swaps the
default movement for that pattern rather than deleting the pattern: knee removes
barbell squats and deep lunges in favour of leg press, split squats to comfort
and leg curls, which is exactly the substitution his own summer and five day
templates already encode.

**C3. The pain rule.** Shown once, when C2 has anything selected. "Working
around it is normal. Keep it at 5 out of 10 or under while you lift, and it
should be back to its usual self the next morning. If it is worse the next day,
drop the load."
Plus one red flag question: "Any of these? Pain that wakes you at night,
numbness or pins and needles down the limb, the joint giving way, a recent bad
fall, unexplained weight loss." Yes shows a get seen first note and stops us
suggesting loading that joint until they clear it.

**C4. Barbell confidence.** "How do you feel about barbell lifts?"
Confident / Done them, a bit rusty / Never tried / Not interested
Selects machine first or barbell first for the big patterns. Framed as taste,
because the outcomes are equivalent and the machine version is the lower skill
entry point, not the lesser one.

**C5. The rest of the week.** "Anything else in your week?" Running / Cycling /
Sport / Classes / Nothing
Only changes advice: lift first when both fall on one day, and if running is
heavy and legs are stalling, that is the first place to look.

**C6. Age band.** Optional, offered in settings. Under 40 / 40 to 59 / 60+
60+ adds one fast, deliberate movement per session and pins the default at 2 to
3 sets per exercise, per the 2026 older adult recommendations.

**C7. Dislikes.** "Anything you hate doing?" multi-select over the library.
Pure autonomy play. Also the most useful single field for adherence per session
after days per week.

**C8. Bodyweight.** Optional, needed only for the strength standard comparison
and for bodyweight movement load estimates.

### Tier 3, never asked, derived from the log

- **Effort calibration.** Whether their RPE means anything, inferred from how
  reps decay across sets, not from a question.
- **Current strength.** Read from the first two sessions. Never ask for a 1RM,
  and never make anyone test one to get a program.
- **Volume tolerance.** Planned sets versus completed sets.
- **True frequency.** Distinct workout days per 28 days, compared against Q3.
- **Favourite movements.** What they keep re-picking and repeating.
- **Progression readiness.** Load and rep trend per movement.

## Turning answers into a program

### Experience score

    uninterrupted training time (Q1)   never 0 · under 6mo 1 · 6mo to 2y 2 · over 2y 3
    previous experience (Q2)           none 0 · over a year ago 1 · within a year 2
    barbell confidence (C4, if known)  never tried 0 · rusty 1 · confident 2

    total 0 to 1    beginner
    total 2 to 3    returner if Q1 is never or under 6 months, else intermediate
    total 4 to 5    intermediate
    total 6 plus    advanced

C4 is not asked at first run, so the first pass runs on Q1 and Q2 alone and the
level is revised silently once C4 arrives.

### Split selection, level by days

Every cell names days that already exist in `lib/templates.ts`.

| Days | Beginner | Returner | Intermediate | Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Full Body A, B | Full Body A, B | Upper Lower, Upper A and Lower A | Upper Lower, Upper A and Lower A |
| 3 | Full Body A, B, C | Full Body A, B, C | Push Pull Legs | Push Pull Legs |
| 4 | Upper Lower | Full Body 3 for 2 weeks, then Upper Lower | Upper Lower | Summer 4 Day |
| 5 | Upper Lower plus one repeat, with a note | Upper Lower, build to 5 | 5 Day | 5 Day |
| 6 | not offered, 4 suggested instead | not offered | Push Pull Legs twice through | Push Pull Legs twice through, or Bro Split on request |

Rules behind the table:

- Beginners are steered to full body because frequency per muscle is high at low
  total volume, and because missing one of three sessions still leaves every
  muscle trained. A missed day on a bro split costs a whole muscle group a week.
- Nobody is given 6 days at beginner level. Offer 4 and say why in one line.
- Bro Split is never suggested, only honoured when asked for. It is not worse
  once weekly volume is matched, but it is the least forgiving of a missed day.
- His Summer 4 Day and 5 Day carry the core circuit and no barbell squat or
  heavy hinge. They suit anyone who flags a knee or low back, which is why they
  sit at the top of the advanced column rather than being personal-only.

### Volume and load defaults

- Target 10 or more sets per muscle per week. At 2 days a week that means 4 to 5
  sets per muscle per session, at 5 days it spreads to 2 to 3.
- Beginner: 2 to 3 sets per exercise, 8 to 12 reps, stop 2 to 3 reps short.
- Intermediate and advanced: the existing goal ranges, strength 3 to 6, muscle
  6 to 12, endurance 12 to 20.
- Nobody is told to train to failure by default. Near failure matches failure for
  growth and strength with less fatigue.
- Beginners see no RPE field. Progression is "same load, one more rep than last
  time, and add load when you clear the top of the range twice".

### Skips and defaults

Every question is skippable and skipping is silent. Full skip gives: 3 days,
Full Body A, B, C, muscle goal, full gym, 60 minutes, no RPE field. That
combination is defensible for almost anyone who answered no to the gate.

## What the system should watch instead of asking

- **Distinct workout days in the first 28 days.** The strongest adherence signal
  there is. Under 8 by day 28 is the cue to shorten sessions or drop a day, not
  to add anything.
- **Weeks 6 to 10.** The known dropout window. This is where a streak, a PR
  callout or a deliberately easy session earns its keep.
- **Planned versus completed sets.** Three sessions in a row cut short means the
  session is too long, whatever they answered to C1.
- **Q3 versus reality.** If they said 5 and log 2, change the plan quietly to a
  2 day split rather than showing three red misses a week.

## What not to ask, and why

- **"Are you a beginner, intermediate or advanced?"** Self labels are noise and
  the label itself demotivates. Ask time and history instead, and never show the
  resulting badge.
- **1RM or any max test.** Nobody should have to test a max to get a first
  session, and asking implies barbell literacy we have not established.
- **Body fat, measurements, before photos.** Not needed to pick a split, and the
  most common reason a fitness onboarding gets abandoned on the first screen.
- **Full medical history.** We are not a clinic. Two gate questions plus the red
  flag probe when pain is flagged is the honest limit of what a log book should
  claim to do.
- **"How motivated are you, 1 to 10?"** Predicts nothing we can act on. Logged
  days predict everything.
- **Diet.** Unless and until the app does anything with the answer.

## Open questions for you

   questions and a single line of terms, given the app prescribes nothing. My

2. Bodyweight in pounds only, or a unit setting? Everything else in the app is
